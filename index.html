Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about Mentoobeing.

Clear answers about what Mentoobeing does, what its assessments mean and when professional support may be appropriate.

What is Mentoobeing?

+

Mentoobeing is a student-first mental wellbeing platform that provides educational information, self-reflection tools, practical wellbeing guidance and links to trusted resources. It is designed to help students understand patterns in their wellbeing and choose small, realistic next steps.

Are Mentoobeing assessments a diagnosis?

+

No. The Basic Mental Wellbeing Assessment and Advanced Mental Wellness Test are original educational self-reflection tools. They are not clinically validated screening tests and cannot diagnose depression, anxiety or another mental health condition.

How can students improve their mental wellbeing?

+

Small, repeatable actions can support wellbeing, such as protecting sleep, moving regularly, getting daylight, taking breaks from repetitive scrolling, maintaining supportive connections and breaking overwhelming tasks into smaller steps. Different people need different kinds of support.

What should I do if I am struggling for a long time?

+

If difficult feelings or changes in sleep, energy, concentration, mood or daily functioning persist, worsen or interfere with everyday life, consider speaking with a qualified mental health professional or doctor. Mentoobeing is educational and is not a replacement for professional care.

How can I reduce doom-scrolling?

+

Start with one small change: turn off non-essential notifications, move distracting apps away from the home screen, create phone-free study blocks, try grayscale and keep the phone away from your bed at night. The goal is to make automatic checking less convenient rather than aiming for perfection.

Is Mentoobeing an emergency or therapy service?

+

No. Mentoobeing does not provide emergency care, therapy, diagnosis or medical treatment. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department. In India, the website also provides information about Tele-MANAS.
